\chapter{Results and Discussion}
\label{ch:results-discussion}

This chapter presents experimental results and discussion following the two-phase hybrid
methodology defined in Section~\ref{sec:methodology}: Phase~A
(Section~\ref{sec:phase-a-results}) benchmarks centralized vs.\ federated path
loss regression in software simulation; Phase~B
(Section~\ref{sec:phase-b-results}) validates the system on real
MKR~WAN~1310 hardware. Finally, the results are discussed in detail, highlighting
key findings, limitations, and comparisons with prior work.

%%==========================================================================%%
\\section{Phase A: Simulation-Based Benchmarking Results}
\label{sec:phase-a-results}

The Phase~A simulation was executed on a Windows~11 workstation with a Conda environment (Python~3.13, TensorFlow~2.21.0). All models used the identical Dense($9{\to}8{\to}1$) architecture (89~trainable parameters, 356~bytes of float32 weights). Reproducibility was ensured by fixing the random seed to~42 for NumPy and TensorFlow. The total simulation runtimes were approximately 15--20 minutes for first-order algorithms (FedAvg, FedProx), 30 minutes for SCAFFOLD, and 92.6 minutes for FedDyn due to the eager execution of the custom regularizer gradient tape.

\subsection{Centralized Baseline}
\label{sec:centralized-baseline}
A centralized Dense($9{\to}8{\to}1$) model was trained on the merged training set (1,663,622~samples, 80\,\% of 2,079,528) with the Adam optimizer (lr~$=$~0.001), MSE loss, batch size~$=$~2{,}048, and early stopping (patience~$=$~8, monitoring validation loss, restoring best weights). A separate 15\,\% validation split was carved from the training set for early stopping. The model was evaluated on a held-out test set of 415,906~samples (20\,\%).

\noindent\textbf{Result:}
\[
  R^2 = 0.9205, \quad
  \text{RMSE} = 5.320\;\text{dB}, \quad
  \text{MAE} = 3.947\;\text{dB}.
\]

The centralized model achieves high accuracy by mapping the spatial characteristics (distance, brick/wood wall counts), link quality (SNR), and local environmental parameters (temperature, humidity, pressure, $\text{CO}_2$, $\text{PM}_{2.5}$) to the expected path loss. This establishes the \emph{upper bound} against which all federated configurations are compared.

Figure~\ref{fig:central-loss} shows the training and validation loss curves. Both curves decrease rapidly and converge within the first 10~epochs. Figure~\ref{fig:pred-vs-actual} presents the predicted vs.\ actual path loss scatter plot and residual distribution, illustrating tight alignment along the identity line and a narrow zero-centered residual histogram.

\subsection{Federated Learning Convergence (FedAvg)}
\label{sec:fl-convergence}
The \gls{fl} simulation used \gls{fedavg} with all six clients participating in every round ($K = 6$). Three local-epoch configurations ($E \in \{1, 3, 5\}$) were evaluated over $N = 20$ communication rounds each. The global model is evaluated on the held-out test set after each round.

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{ccccc}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Local Epochs ($E$)} & \textbf{$R^2$} & \textbf{RMSE (dB)} & \textbf{MAE (dB)} & \textbf{$R^2$ / Centralized} \\
    \midrule
    1  & $-0.6869$  & 24.50  & 19.75  & --- (not converged) \\
    3  & 0.6109     & 11.77  & 9.51   & 66.37\,\% \\
    5  & \textbf{0.6682} & \textbf{10.87} & \textbf{8.74} & \textbf{72.59\,\%} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Federated learning (FedAvg) results after 20 communication rounds with 6~clients (non-IID split by physical device location).}
  \label{tab:fl-results}
\end{table}

With $E=1$ local epoch, the model fails to converge within 20~rounds (negative $R^2$), indicating insufficient local training per round to overcome random weight initialization under non-IID conditions. At $E=3$, the federated model achieves $R^2 = 0.6109$. At $E=5$, convergence reaches $R^2 = 0.6682$ and RMSE~$= 10.87$\,dB by round~20. The gap between the best FedAvg model ($R^2 = 0.6682$) and the centralized baseline ($R^2 = 0.9205$) represents the penalty of client drift induced by the severe spatial heterogeneity of the non-IID room distributions.

Figure~\ref{fig:fl-convergence} illustrates the convergence behaviour. The left panel shows $R^2$ vs.\ communication round for each local-epoch setting, and the right panel shows the corresponding RMSE convergence.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig4_fl_convergence_r2_rmse.png}
  \caption{Federated learning convergence (FedAvg): $R^2$ (left) and RMSE (right) vs.\ communication round for $E \in \{1, 3, 5\}$ local epochs. The black dashed line marks the centralized baseline ($R^2 = 0.9205$); the grey dashed line marks the supervisor's LDPLSM-MW-EP result ($R^2 = 0.8219$).}
  \label{fig:fl-convergence}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Comparison of Federated Optimization Algorithms}
\label{sec:algorithm-comparison}
To mitigate the client drift observed in FedAvg, four advanced federated optimization algorithms were implemented and evaluated under identical conditions. Table~\ref{tab:comparison-algorithms} summarizes the best test metrics achieved by each algorithm after 20 rounds.

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{lccccc}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Algorithm} & \textbf{Local Epochs ($E$)} & \textbf{$R^2$} & \textbf{RMSE (dB)} & \textbf{MAE (dB)} & \textbf{Convergence State} \\
    \midrule
    \textbf{Centralized NN} & --- & \textbf{0.9205} & \textbf{5.32} & \textbf{3.95} & Upper Bound \\
    \midrule
    \textbf{SCAFFOLD} & $E=3$ & \textbf{0.8619} & \textbf{7.01} & \textbf{5.15} & Stable Convergence \\
    \textbf{FedProx} & $E=5$ & 0.6781 & 10.70 & 8.53 & Stable Convergence \\
    \textbf{FedAvg} & $E=5$ & 0.6682 & 10.87 & 8.74 & Stable Convergence \\
    \textbf{FedDyn} & $E=3$ & 0.4830 & 13.57 & 10.64 & Oscillatory (Unstable $E=5$) \\
    \textbf{FedYogi} & $E=1$ & $-2.5206$ & 35.40 & 28.49 & Divergent \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Comparison of federated optimization algorithms after 20 communication rounds on the non-IID indoor LoRaWAN dataset.}
  \label{tab:comparison-algorithms}
\end{table}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{SCAFFOLD (Winner):} Achieving $R^2 = 0.8619$ and RMSE~$= 7.01$\,dB at $E=3$, SCAFFOLD significantly outperforms all other federated configurations. By utilizing stateful control variates to track and offset the direction of local client updates, it effectively corrects the gradient drift caused by spatial heterogeneity, recovering 93.6\% of the centralized model's performance.
    \item \textbf{FedProx:} Achieves a marginal improvement over the FedAvg baseline ($R^2 = 0.6781$ vs. $0.6682$) at $E=5$. The proximal regularization term ($\mu = 0.01$) successfully restricts local updates from straying too far from the global consensus, but the isotropic penalty is insufficient to fully correct the directional gradient drift.
    \item \textbf{FedDyn:} Achieves stable convergence only at $E=3$ ($R^2 = 0.4830$, RMSE~$= 13.57$\,dB). At $E=5$, the dynamic regularization penalty introduces severe second-order parameter oscillations around the global consensus, leading to update instability and causing the test $R^2$ to degrade to $-1.9689$.
    \item \textbf{FedYogi:} Fails to converge, yielding a negative $R^2$ of $-2.5206$ at $E=1$. While adaptive server optimizers are highly effective for large models, the server-side learning rate ($\eta_s = 0.01$) and adaptive variance momentum overshot the global optimum for this tiny 89-parameter network under non-IID split constraints.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Effect of Non-IID Data Distribution}
\label{sec:non-iid-effect}
The naturally non-IID spatial data split is a major challenge for local model training. To analyze this, the best federated global models were evaluated on the local data partitions of each client individually. Figure~\ref{fig:per-client-r2} shows the per-client $R^2$ performance for the leading federated algorithms. While FedAvg and FedProx show high variation due to local room characteristics, SCAFFOLD maintains a high and balanced generalization performance across all six end devices, indicating that its gradient correction vector successfully learned spatial-agnostic features.

\subsection{Communication Efficiency (RQ3)}
\label{sec:comm-efficiency}
The daily network bandwidth consumption of the federated edge scheme was analyzed against the traditional centralized telemetry architecture:

\noindent\textbf{Old system (raw data):} Each node transmits a status message every 60~seconds (1{,}440~messages/day) containing 18~bytes of raw sensor data. Daily cost: $1{,}440 \times 18 = 25{,}920$\,B per node.

\noindent\textbf{New system (federated):} Each node transmits a compressed 8-byte status message every 5~minutes (288~messages/day $\times$ 8\,B $=$ 2{,}304\,B), plus one 89-byte FL weight update per round per day (89\,B). Daily cost: $2{,}304 + 89 = 2{,}393$\,B per node.


\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{lrr}
    \toprule
    & \textbf{Old --- Raw Data} & \textbf{New --- Federated} \\
    \midrule
    Payload / message     & 18\,B          & 8\,B (status) / 52\,B (FL update) \\
    Messages / day        & 1{,}440        & 288 (status) $+$ 1 (FL)           \\
    Bytes / day / node    & 25{,}920       & 2{,}356                           \\
    Bytes / day / 6 nodes & 155{,}520      & 14{,}136                          \\
    \textbf{Reduction}    & ---            & $\boldsymbol{\approx 11.0\times}$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Daily communication cost per node: prior raw-data transmission vs.\
    federated update scheme.}
  \label{tab:bandwidth}
\end{table}

\noindent
For comparison, Torres Sanchez et al.'s 1.39\,KB autoencoder required
$\lceil 1{,}390 / 51 \rceil = 28$ \gls{lorawan} messages per round per client at SF12. Our 52-byte update requires exactly \textbf{one} message per round at any spreading factor---a direct consequence of the $\approx$\,27$\times$ smaller model size. \textbf{RQ3 is answered:} the federated scheme reduces daily communication overhead by $\approx$11$\times$.

Figure~\ref{fig:comm-efficiency} visualizes this comparison on a logarithmic scale.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.75\textwidth]{fig7_communication_efficiency.png}
  \caption{Communication efficiency comparison (bytes/node/day, log scale). The old raw-data system transmits 25{,}920\,B/day vs.\ the federated system's 2{,}356\,B/day---an $\approx$11$\times$ reduction. The Torres Sanchez FL update (1{,}428\,B per round) is shown for reference.}
  \label{fig:comm-efficiency}
\end{figure}

\section{Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Simplified local training:} Weight proxy array, not true TFLite backpropagation.
  \item \textbf{Simulation-based FL:} Data is real but FL process is simulated (standard practice---Torres Sanchez et al.\ also used simulation).
  \item \textbf{Single building, single gateway:} Results may not generalize.
  \item \textbf{Proxy labels:} On-device uses \gls{pdr} as proxy, not direct path loss.
  \item \textbf{Fixed architecture:} Only Dense($8{\to}8{\to}1$) evaluated.
\end{enumerate}
